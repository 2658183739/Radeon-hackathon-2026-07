## Summary

### Overview

The two searches cover 44 API-returned records from 2023-2026 plus eight
foundational additions. The corpus spans generalist VLA construction, common
datasets and benchmarks, contact-rich sensing, self-correction, action-chunk
execution, and inference efficiency. API coverage was incomplete for Query A
because arXiv, DBLP, and Crossref failed; this limitation must remain visible.

### Trends

Work shifted from scaling generalist policies and cross-embodiment datasets in
2023-2024 toward deployment reliability in 2025-2026. Recurring themes are
smaller or distilled policies, action-chunk speedups, asynchronous control,
heterogeneous contact sensing, physical-feasibility critics, and environment-
feedback improvement. LIBERO remains a common simulation benchmark, while DROID,
Open X-Embodiment, BridgeData V2, and ALOHA-style data support real-robot transfer.
Recent work increasingly separates model throughput from closed-loop success and
adds physical or safety checks rather than treating offline action loss as enough.

### Key Themes

1. **Generalist policy and dataset scaling.** OpenVLA, Octo, DROID, RT-2, and
   Open X-Embodiment establish the common pretraining and transfer setting
   (Query B #4, #5, #23; MK1-MK3).
2. **Contact-aware execution.** Tactile/contact fusion and physical-feasibility
   regulation target failures that RGB-only policies miss during grasp, hold,
   transport, and release (Query A #1, #2, #4, #6, #8).
3. **Temporal execution and efficiency.** Action chunks, parallel decoding,
   delay-aware adapters, policy distillation, and smaller VLAs trade inference
   cost against reactivity (Query A #9, #12, #14; Query B #1, #8, #10).
4. **Verified correction and adaptation.** Self-correcting, self-reflective,
   world-model, and environment-feedback methods attempt improvement after
   deployment (Query A #2, #3, #5, #11; Query B #16, #17).
5. **Benchmark and safety expansion.** LIBERO, LIBERO-Safety, RoboDojo, and
   task-specific 3D benchmarks broaden evaluation beyond a single success
   number (Query B #6, #14, #21; MK5).

### Recommendations for Reading

1. **Octo (Query B #5)** for the generalist-policy and action-chunk baseline.
2. **OpenVLA (Query B #4)** for open VLA fine-tuning and embodiment transfer.
3. **Fine-Tuning VLAs: Optimizing Speed and Success (Query B #1)** for the
   direct accuracy/latency design tradeoff relevant to Radeon deployment.
4. **TacVLA (Query A #4)** for contact-aware inputs and contact-stage metrics.
5. **A Self-Correcting VLA (Query A #11)** for a correction loop that can be
   compared against this project's stricter replay-verification boundary.
6. **PD-VLA (Query A #12)** for action-chunk parallelism, with success and energy
   non-regression required before adopting any runtime change.
